Calibration

Our chainage runs short of the official posts, by one metre per kilometre

Thirty milestones on line 70 carry a railway:position — the real MÁV kilometre. Comparing them against the chainage measured off the geometry gives a clean, almost perfectly linear drift: mean -24.3 m, reaching about −47 m by km 50.

Measured chainage minus official km post

This is not an error, it is the expected thing. Kilometre posts were planted along the alignment as it was, and have not moved since; the track under them has been realigned, curves eased, the 1971–73 reconstruction increased radii. Our measurement follows today's rails. Both numbers are right about different things.

What it means for the game: display official kilometres, because that is what a driver reads off the lineside and what the timetable is written in. Drive on measured chainage, because that is what the wheels do. The fit above converts between them.

Signalling

Enough to anchor a block plan, not enough to be one

91 signals sit on line 70 itself, 53 of them tagged as main signals. By function: 20 block signals (térközjelző), 12 entry (bejárati), 12 exit (kijárati), and 45 mapped as a signal and nothing more.

A double-track automatic-block railway of this length, worked at 120 and 100 km/h, needs somewhere around 158 main signals. We have about a third of them. The longest gap between two mapped signals is 16.5 km, which is not a block section, it is a hole in the survey.

So the honest plan is

Treat the mapped signals as fixed anchors — they are real positions and must not move — and synthesise the rest between them, spaced by braking distance at line speed. Where OSM gives us an entry or exit signal at a station, that station's approach is already correct. Median measured gap on the down line is 800 m, but that number is pulled down by station entry/exit pairs; the free-running block sections should come out nearer 1500–1800 m.

Formation

How many tracks, and where they sit

Each dot is a track centre, measured as a perpendicular offset from the down line and clustered so that a track split into a dozen OSM fragments still counts once. Read across and you get the shape of the railway: two roads almost everywhere, opening at stations, and a long three-track stretch after Vác where line 75 runs alongside.

Track centres across the formation · signals · level crossings

The wide places are exactly where they should be. Rákosrendező at km 3.3 is sixteen roads at a regular 4.7 m spacing — the old hump yard. Dunakeszi at 14.1 is eight, for the carriage works. Vác reaches nine, Szob ten. Nagymaros holds a steady six through the station. Kismaros, despite being the junction for the narrow gauge, is two: it is a megállóhely, a halt, and the forest railway does not connect to it.

Which line is which

70, 71 and 75 share more track than the map suggests

Every rail way carries a ref naming the line it belongs to, and reading them by station settles something the route diagram only hints at:

Rákospalota-Újpest
70 + 71 — line 71 to Vácrátót leaves here.
Vác-Alsóváros
70 + 71 — and rejoins here, having gone the long way round through Fót and Vácrátót.
Vác
70 + 71 + 75 — three lines. This is the operational centre of the whole route.
Kisvác and Fenyveshegy
70 + 75 — the two lines run side by side from Vác to just short of Verőce. Both halts are on the 75 tracks; no line 70 train has ever called at either, but you pass their platforms at 100 km/h.
Szob
70 + 120A — the border station, with the connection toward Štúrovo.
Rákosrendező
70 + 209, 210, 218, 219, 220 — five yard and connecting line numbers on top of the main line.

Three independent sources now agree on the Vác–Verőce parallel running: the Wikipedia route diagram marks Kisvác and Fenyveshegy "csak 75", the station nodes snapped to within four metres of our centreline, and the track formation reads three roads from km 34.5 to 41.5.

Switches and crossings

Where the line can be got at

109 switches sit on the running lines, out of 618 in the corridor. 53 carry a diverging speed and the answer is almost always the same: 40 km/h, which is the standard Hungarian turnout speed and the reason 40 is one of the aspects a signal can show. 47 are electrically worked; 37 are heated.

Structures

Bridges and tunnels

83 structures within 140 m of the down line — 58 bridges, 25 tunnels, 1629 m of bridged track in total.

Two are worth naming now. At km 63.46 the Sávoly híd carries the line over the Ipoly into Slovakia, and OSM has it twice — once as Sávoly híd and once as Sávolyho most, which is the border in a single data field. And at km 57.97 there is a pair of 75 m spans immediately before Zebegény, which is where the Hétlyukú híd stands: seven arches of 7.5 m built in 1850, still carrying traffic. OSM does not name it, so treat that identification as very likely rather than confirmed.

Gaps

What we still need, in priority order

Asked to think about what else is wanted. This is the honest list, ordered by how much it blocks.

Blocking the simulator

Platform numbers
Not in OSM at all. No station on the line has its roads numbered. You cannot say "arrive on road 3 at Vác" without them, and the dispatcher panel needs them on every siding. Source: MÁV station diagrams, or platform photographs. This is the single biggest hole.
Two thirds of the signals
Synthesise between the anchors, spaced by braking distance. Needs a decision on section length, which needs the next item.
F.1 signalling rules
Which aspects may follow which, approach release, what the előjelző shows for each main aspect. Without it the signals are lamps, not a system.
Full timetable
We have S70 minutes. G70, Z70, the four international services and the freight paths all need real times, or the traffic conflicts are invented.
KISS vehicle data
Mass, tractive effort curve, braking rates, length, door positions. From Stadler's published spec, not OSM.

Blocking the look

Land cover
Fetched today: 2874 polygons within 2.5 km — 863 forest, 538 scrub, 322 meadow, 247 wood, 86 orchard, 53 vineyard, 70 cliff, 58 bare rock. That is the vegetation scatter, already sourced. Not yet processed.
Buildings
Station buildings and settlement footprints for the diorama. In OSM, not yet pulled — it is a big fetch and wants doing once.
Curvature and cant
Radius is computable from the geometry we have. Cant is not mapped anywhere and affects how the train sits in a curve.
Catenary
Mast positions are partly tagged. Regular spacing is a reasonable fake; the wire is very visible from a cab.

Worth having, not urgent

Királyréti Erdei Vasút
38 narrow-gauge ways are already sitting in the corridor data, unprocessed. Twelve kilometres, 760 mm, Kismaros to Királyrét.
Danube water level
The river is the view for thirty kilometres and its level moves several metres a year.
Weather normals
Fog days, rain days, first and last frost for the Bend, so the weather you asked for is the weather that actually happens here.
Sun position
Computable, no source needed — but worth doing early, because it decides when the sunsets you want actually land.
