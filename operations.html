You were right

There are no tunnels on line 70

I intersected every structure with the roads and watercourses around it and let the geometry decide what it is. Of 25 rail ways tagged as tunnel, 24 carry tunnel=building_passage — the tag for a railway running under a building or a canopy, not through rock. The remaining three are railway=abandoned, one of them charmingly named Felszedett Gazdasági vasút vágánya, "lifted industrial railway track". None of them is on the operational line.

The fourteen in the first kilometre out of Nyugati are the train shed and the structures around Podmaniczky utca. Nineteen of the twenty-four have nothing crossing overhead at all, which is exactly what you would expect of track under a roof. Your instinct was better than my table.

And the bridges are mostly streams, not roads

Partly right on this one. Of the bridges, seventeen span only a road and four only a path — so yes, plenty are just road overbridges. But the rest cross water, and the names that came back are a complete cross-check against the route diagram you saved: Szilas-patak, Csömöri-patak, Mogyoródi-patak, Ilka-patak, Sződrákos-patak, Gombás-patak, Felső-Gombás-patak, Morgó-patak, Mosoni-patak, Ernő-patak, Malom-patak. Every one of those appears in the Wikipedia jelmagyarázat, in the right order, at the right distance.

And it settles the Hétlyukú híd

At km 57.97 and 58.04 there is a pair of 75 m spans crossing the Malom-patak and Árpád utca, immediately before Zebegény. The route diagram lists "Hétlyukú híd (Malomvölgyi-patak)" at exactly that point. Last pass I could only call it likely. The watercourse name confirms it: seven arches of 7.5 m, built 1850, still carrying you.

Topology

The disconnected tracks — three different reasons

You were reading a real flaw. The old diagrams joined track centres that happened to sit near each other, which invented connections and broke real ones. These are rebuilt from OSM node identity: two ways are joined if and only if they share a node.

Rebuilt that way, most stations come back as a single connected component — Rákospalota-Újpest, Dunakeszi, Göd, Vác, Verőce, Nagymaros-Visegrád and Nagymaros are all one piece. Where something is isolated, it is isolated for one of three reasons, and only one of them is a data problem.

Reason 1 · different gauge

It physically cannot connect

Kismaros: seven railway=narrow_gauge ways, running away from the station up the valley. That is the Királyréti Erdei Vasút at 760 mm. Szob: ten more, the remains of the Nagybörzsöny forest railway. No train can pass between them and the main line, so OSM is right to leave them apart. Drawn in yellow below.

Reason 2 · the connection was lifted

It used to connect and no longer does

Vác-Alsóváros: three industrial spurs and a yard road, 10 to 130 m off the line at km 30.8–32.5. That is the Forte photochemical works and the Tungsram siding. Forte closed in 2007 and the junction has gone. A second group of three yard roads on the other side is the timber loading area. Genuinely detached — and worth modelling as rusted stubs, because that is what is there.

Reason 3 · my window, not the railway

It connects just outside the frame

Rákosrendező: two usage=main ways running from −24 m out to −1235 m. Those are the Esztergom line and the körvasút heading for Rákos. They join the network perfectly well, 1.2 km away, outside my ±850 m extract. Zebegény is the same story in miniature: its up line shows as a separate component because there is no crossover at a megállóhely — and that is not a fault, it is the operational truth. Two running lines that never touch between stations.

What it means for the sim

Component count tells you what kind of place this is

A stop whose ways form one component has points and can be worked as a station. A stop whose two running lines stay separate is a halt: trains call, nothing can be crossed over, and the dispatcher has no moves to make there. That distinction falls straight out of the graph and it is exactly what the panel needs to know.

The block plan

Two thirds synthesised, one third fixed

Mapped signals are anchors and do not move. Everything between them is filled in at a spacing set by line speed, never closer than the MÁV standard braking distance (fékúttávolság) of 700 m. The result is 22+34 signals on the down line and 23+34 on the up.

Block plan · dotted = synthesised, dot = mapped anchor

Braking distance
700 m — the MÁV standard, and the hard floor for any running block.
Section targets
1500 m at 120 km/h, 1300 at 100, 1100 at 80, 900 at 60. Median achieved: 1315 m down, 1323 m up.
Station sections
Seven on the down line, ten on the up, from 109 to 626 m. These sit between an entry and an exit signal and are platform roads, not running blocks, so the braking-distance floor does not apply.
Still too short
Two running blocks on the down line at 361 m and one on the up at 381 m, all OSM anchor pairs just outside station limits. Left alone rather than moved, since the anchors are real.
Theoretical headway
103 s at 120 km/h with 1500 m sections. Ignore it — what actually limits this line is Nyugati's platforms, the flat junction at Rákospalota-Újpest and 50 s dwells. Plan for three to four minutes.

In the cab

EVM-120 repeats the next signal's permitted speed into the cab through coded track circuits, so the driver sees 120, 80, 40, 15 or 0 before the signal is in sight. A restrictive change has to be acknowledged; ignore it and the brake applies. Over the top of that sits the éberségi berendezés, the vigilance device — the same dial as the SIFA in your ZUSI screenshot. Two things to watch and one button that will stop your train if you stop paying attention. That is the tactical layer, and it is real.

Timings

The physics reproduces the timetable

Rather than guess at running times, I built a train. MÁV 815 at 314 t tare plus load, 400 kN starting tractive effort, 4000 kW continuous, 1.1 m/s² acceleration, 0.9 m/s² service braking, against our own gradient profile and OSM speed limits, integrated in 20 m steps with a backward braking pass.

Simulated S70 against the published timetable

Driven flat out to the limits, a KISS covers Nyugati to Szob in 52.0 minutes of pure running. The published all-stations time is 77. Fitting two parameters — recovery margin and station dwell — to the twenty published times, holding dwell to a range a real stop actually takes, gives 16.5% recovery margin and 50 s dwell, and reproduces every one of the twenty station times to within 1.8 minutes, rms 0.87.

The check that was not fitted

That model implies an average speed of 49.8 km/h over the route. The published figure for the S70 is 49–53 km/h — a number that went nowhere near the fit. The vehicle model, the gradients and the speed profile are all right at once.

This is where the game lives. The timetable is 1.48× the technical minimum. A driver who reads the road well arrives early and waits; a driver who dawdles loses a path. And because the Z70 covers the route in 55 minutes against the S70's 76, an all-stations train that runs late at Sződ-Sződliget is in the way of something much faster by Vác. That conflict is not designed, it falls out of the real timetable and the real physics.
